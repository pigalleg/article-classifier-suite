# Alignment of IEEE Transactions on Power Systems with the 2025 Research Agenda

## IV. Results

### A. Overall Engagement with the Research Agenda

We analysed 6,005 papers published in IEEE *Transactions on Power Systems* (TPWRS) between 2015 and August 2026, with an annual output ranging from 358 to 703 papers. Each paper was scored against the 48 questions of the 2025 Research Agenda (RA2025), and its coverage of each of the six primary research programmes was determined from its highest-affinity question within that programme. All results are reported as a share of the papers published in the same period, so that the pronounced variation in annual journal output does not confound the temporal comparison.

Fig. 1 shows the share of papers reaching each of two nested thresholds. Engagement with the agenda is broad: across the full period, 83.8% of papers reached at least Moderate affinity with one or more programmes. This series remains high in every year, ranging from 78.7% to 91.2%. The High-affinity series is lower but rises in the later years: it increases from 14.5% in 2015 to 23.2% in 2025, reaching 33.5% in the partial 2026 sample, with a period-wide share of 20.1%.

Taken descriptively, the changing profile is chiefly one of stronger alignment rather than wider initial contact. Moderate-or-better contact was already common at the beginning of the period, whereas the share of papers with a High match increases from the mid-teens in 2015-2017 to around one quarter in 2023-2025. The 2026 observation should be read cautiously because it covers January to August only.

### B. Distribution Across Research Programmes

Fig. 2 disaggregates coverage by programme as six affinity-level shares for each publication year; each stacked area sums to 100% of that year's TPWRS papers. The top-left annotation in each panel reports the pooled 2015-2026 share at the Moderate-or-better and High-or-better thresholds. The distribution is markedly uneven. Ordered by period-wide Moderate-or-better share, CROF is the most widely covered programme at 60.6%, followed by Stability (49.9%), Planning (35.9%), System Services (29.3%), IBR (24.5%) and DER (21.3%). These shares are not additive, since a single paper may reach several programmes.

At the High-or-better threshold, every programme remains below 6% (CROF 5.7%, Stability 5.2%, Planning 4.4%, System Services 4.0%, IBR 2.2%, DER 2.0%). The separate High+ band in the stacked distribution identifies the small subset at the strictest affinity level. Close alignment with a specific programme question is thus roughly an order of magnitude rarer than broad thematic relevance, and no programme dominates the strongly aligned literature. This contrast indicates that the agenda's questions are considerably more specific than the typical scope of a TPWRS contribution, so that most papers touch a programme's subject area without addressing its stated question directly.

### C. Structural Shift Between Eras

Single-year shares vary with the number of papers published each year. Fig. 3 and Table I therefore pool the corpus into four three-year eras containing 1,387 to 1,697 papers, making the broad changes easier to compare.

The expansion is concentrated rather than uniform. Stability has the largest Moderate+ increase, from 41.2% in 2015-2017 to 58.9% in 2024-2026 (+17.6 percentage points (pp)); its High share rises from 2.5% to 9.7% (+7.2 pp). CROF, IBR and System Services also broaden at Moderate+, by +12.0 pp, +10.9 pp and +8.4 pp respectively. CROF increases from 56.9% to 68.8%, IBR from 18.7% to 29.6%, and System Services from 23.6% to 32.1%.

Planning and DER show a different descriptive profile. Planning changes little between the first and last era: Moderate+ coverage moves from 38.4% to 37.3% (-1.1 pp), while High coverage changes from 4.1% to 4.0% (-0.1 pp). DER broadens only slightly at Moderate+, from 18.5% to 21.8% (+3.3 pp), although its High share rises from 1.2% to 2.5% (+1.3 pp). For DER, the annual endpoints can be misleading: Moderate+ coverage is 23.7% in 2015 and 19.0% in the partial 2026 sample, whereas the pooled-era comparison points in the opposite direction. The pooled estimates are therefore the more useful descriptive summary of this fluctuating series.

### D. Discussion

Taken together, the three figures show that TPWRS has sustained broad contact with the 2025 Research Agenda, alongside a larger share of strongly aligned papers in the later years. The descriptive increase is most apparent for Stability, CROF and IBR, consistent with the growing prominence of converter-interfaced generation and associated stability challenges. System Services also reaches a larger share of papers in the later eras, whereas DER and Planning are comparatively stable in breadth.

Planning is the clearest descriptive gap. It is the third most widely covered programme overall, but its Moderate+ and High shares are almost unchanged between the first and last pooled eras while several other programmes expand. Two limitations qualify these findings. First, the 2026 sample is partial and its annual value should not be interpreted as a full-year estimate. Second, coverage measured from each paper's single best-matching question quantifies how many papers a programme reaches, not the depth of work each paper contributes; programmes comprising many narrowly scoped questions are therefore not disadvantaged by dilution, but neither is intensity captured.
